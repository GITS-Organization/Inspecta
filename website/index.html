Inspection work breaks down in the gaps between people.

Paper, spreadsheets and chat threads each hold a piece of the job. Inspecta keeps one record that every role works from.

The challenge How Inspecta handles it

  1. Paper-based inspection records

    Inspectors complete a guided digital report: examination details, item information, examination questions, defects and conclusion. It autosaves as a draft and renders to a PDF.

    No re-typing from paper, and the same form every time.

    See the examination report

  2. Disorganized schedules and assignments

    The coordinator sees accepted jobs by due date, picks a date and start time, and assigns an inspector. A Best match tag marks inspectors qualified for the equipment, and free, busy and on-leave states are visible.

    Assignments are made with availability and qualifications in view.

    See assignment

  3. Delayed reporting of defects

    Defects, danger flags and required repairs are recorded in the report itself. When the inspector submits, the job moves straight into the technical manager's review queue.

    Findings reach the reviewer the moment the inspector submits.

    See review and sign

  4. Limited visibility into progress

    Every job carries one status across the six-step lifecycle, with a history of who changed it and when. Each role's home screen counts what needs attention.

    Anyone can see where a job stands without asking.

    See the job lifecycle

  5. Follow-up that lives in email

    The report records the repair, renewal or alteration required and the date a potential danger must be fixed by. A conditional result reads Safe with conditions (fix in 14 days), and the next examination date is recorded.

    Follow-up deadlines sit in the signed record instead of an email.

    See the defects section

  6. Scattered documents and history

    Requests, quotation history, client contacts and equipment on record sit in one place, and each role can search its own work by client, equipment or ID.

    The history you need is one search away.

    See clients

  7. Time-consuming manual reporting

    The certificate PDF is generated from what the inspector entered. Inspectors log time and transport costs per task, the price is worked out from their hourly rate, and a coordinator approves or returns the entry.

    Less copying between documents, with costs recorded as the work happens.

Requests inbox

Every client request lands in one inbox, whether it came from email or was entered by hand. New requests and anything waiting on a reply rise to the top, and you can search by client, equipment or ID.

The problem
Requests buried in a shared mailbox, with no sense of what is waiting or for how long.
What you get
A clear queue: requests left in New for more than 24 hours are flagged, and a client reply is visible at a glance.

Request and job lifecycle

An open request shows the client, location, preferred date, access notes and equipment items with capacity and quantity, plus the six-step tracker: Request, Quote, Assign, Inspect, Certify, Sent. The original email is one tap away.

The problem
Job details scattered across emails, with no shared view of where the job stands.
What you get
One shared view of the job. A request cannot be quoted until the client, equipment and location are filled in, so quotes are not built on gaps.

Quote negotiation

Each quote is a numbered version that cannot be edited once sent, with a validity period. When a client counters, you log their price, then accept it, send a new version, or mark the quote declined. Accepting locks the price and hands the job to the coordinator.

The problem
Prices and counter-offers scattered through email replies, so nobody is sure which number was agreed.
What you get
A complete negotiation history and one agreed, locked price. The app records the exchange; the conversation with the client itself still happens over your usual channel.

Clients and equipment

Each client keeps its contacts, the address certificates are sent to, equipment on record, open jobs, and anything falling due in the next 30 days. A new sender not yet matched to a client is flagged so you can match it.

The problem
Client details and equipment lists kept in personal spreadsheets.
What you get
Everyone works from the same client record, and equipment coming due is easy to spot.

Assign an inspector

Accepted quotations queue up by due date. Pick a time, choose an inspector and assign. Inspectors whose qualifications cover the equipment carry a Best match tag, their load is shown, and someone on leave is never the default pick.

The problem
Dispatching by phone call and memory, which wastes time and invites clashes.
What you get
Assignment in a few taps with qualifications and availability in view, and the inspector sees it as a new task in their app.

Team schedule

A day view per inspector on a time axis: booked tasks, who is free all day, who is on leave. Move between days to see the load before you assign.

The problem
No quick way to know who has free time this week.
What you get
The whole team on one screen, so work is spread with the load in view.

My tasks

The inspector sees today's tasks on a day strip, with a banner for a new task from the coordinator and Accept and Decline buttons. Continue certificate takes them back to where they left off.

The problem
Inspectors learn their day through a call or a message.
What you get
A clear task list on the phone, and the coordinator can see whether a task was accepted.

Examination report

The Report of Thorough Examination runs in five steps: examination details, item information, examination questions, defects and remedial action, and conclusion. It autosaves, and the PDF can be previewed before it is submitted to the technical manager.

The problem
Paper forms or office files retyped later, worded differently by each inspector.
What you get
One consistent form filled in on site, and a PDF produced from it. (The PDF is currently issued in English.)

Timesheet

For each accepted task the inspector logs time and how it was spent, plus internal and external transport costs. Net working time excludes unpaid breaks, and the price is worked out from their hourly rate.

The problem
Hours and expenses reconstructed from memory at month end.
What you get
Costs are logged straight after the task, a coordinator approves them, and approved entries lock. The pricing rule is enforced on the server.

Review and sign

The technical manager opens a submitted certificate, reads the summary and result (safe, safe with conditions, not safe), views the full PDF, adds comments, then signs on screen and approves. Or returns it to the inspector with what needs to change.

The problem
Certificates printed, signed and scanned, then sent back for fixes with no written reason.
What you get
Approval with an on-screen signature, or a return with a documented reason. An approved certificate is recorded as sent to the client's certificates email; the email itself is sent outside the app.

What sets Inspecta apart.

The three things that change how a team works most, first. The rest follow.

One job record, six steps

A job carries its status, change history and documents from the first email to the sent certificate, so nobody re-enters data between teams.

Why it matters: anyone can answer “where is this job?” from the same screen.

A guided digital examination report

The report follows the structure of a Report of Thorough Examination: yes or no questions, flags for existing and potential danger, the repair required, and a three-level result. It saves as the inspector works and exports as a PDF.

Why it matters: every report is written to the same structure, so it is quicker to review and easier to compare.

Review and sign, or return with a reason

A certificate does not go to the client before the technical manager has reviewed it. They sign on screen with their name and license number and approve, or return it to the inspector with what must be fixed.

Why it matters: a clear review step that records who approved and when.

Where Inspecta fits.

Inspecta is designed around the examination of industrial equipment, in particular lifts, cranes and pressure equipment. These are the clearest fits.

  • Cranes and lifting equipment

    The challenge: overhead cranes and lifting plant with many items and capacities across sites, each needing its own examination and certificate.

    How Inspecta helps: each equipment item on a request is quoted and certified separately, capacity is recorded, and the certificate ties back to the client.

  • Lifts

    The challenge: periodic examinations of lifts across many buildings, with items such as landing-door interlocks to check.

    How Inspecta helps: Lifts is a qualification an inspector can hold, so assignment can surface who fits, and a reviewer can return a report with a specific request.

  • Pressure equipment and compressors

    The challenge: air compressors and other pressure equipment that need an examination, with working limits and test results on record.

    How Inspecta helps: the report holds manufacturer, model, maximum working rate, serial number and function-check result, and the next examination date is recorded.

  • Industrial and manufacturing plants

    The challenge: a mix of equipment and recurring examinations, with due dates that are hard to track across departments.

    How Inspecta helps: the client record gathers equipment, open jobs and what is coming due in 30 days.

  • Construction and engineering

    The challenge: cranes and lifting plant moving between sites that need current certificates before use.

    How Inspecta helps: each request records location, preferred date and access notes, so the inspector arrives knowing what is needed.

  • Inspection bodies and field service providers

    The challenge: receiving, quoting, scheduling and certifying work with different roles in different tools.

    How Inspecta helps: all four roles in one app, from the requests inbox to the signed certificate and timesheet.

Built around how inspection work actually moves.

Inspecta is a mobile-first inspection-management app. It is designed around the real sequence of an inspection job (request, quote, assign, inspect, certify, send) and the people who handle each step: supervisors, coordinators, inspectors and technical managers.

Frequently asked questions.

Answers reflect what the app does today. Anything not covered here we can discuss in a demo.

What is Inspecta?

Inspecta is an inspection-management app. It takes a client's request to examine equipment through quoting, scheduling, a digital examination report, technical review and signature, and records the certificate as sent.

Who is Inspecta designed for?

For teams that examine industrial equipment, whether inspection bodies or in-house inspection departments. Four roles are supported today: supervisors, coordinators, inspectors and technical managers.

How does digital inspection management work?

Each request becomes a job with one status that moves through Request, Quote, Assign, Inspect, Certify and Sent. Each role sees the jobs and actions that belong to it, and every status change is written to the job's history.

Can inspection records include photos and attachments?

The current examination report captures structured details, answers to the examination questions, defects, required repairs, tests carried out and a conclusion, and produces a PDF certificate. Attaching photos and files is not part of the current report. If you need it, tell us when you request a demo.

Can inspections be assigned to team members?

Yes. Coordinators assign accepted jobs to an inspector with a date and start time, with a Best match tag from qualifications and availability shown. The inspector accepts or declines the task.

Does Inspecta support mobile devices?

Inspecta is built phone-first and also runs in a web browser. Ask us about the platforms and distribution available for your organization.

Can management review inspection reports?

Yes. Technical managers have a review queue: they read the summary and full PDF, add comments, then sign and approve, or return the report to the inspector with a reason. Sent certificates are listed for reference. Management-level analytics are not part of the current release.

Does Inspecta support Arabic?

Yes. The interface is available in English and Arabic with a right-to-left layout. The certificate PDF is currently issued in English.

How is access controlled?

Each user signs in with their own account and sees only what their role allows. Permissions are enforced by server-side security rules, not just by hiding things in the interface.
